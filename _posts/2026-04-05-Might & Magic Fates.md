The Might & Magic series is one of the pillars of Western RPG history, and with the announcement of Might & Magic: Fates, Ubisoft is aiming to reclaim the throne. Developed by Ubisoft Shanghai—the studio behind some of the more technical entries in the publisher's catalog—Fates represents a high-budget effort to bring the depth of a traditional AAA open-world RPG to the mobile landscape.

A True First-Person Open World
While recent spin-offs in the franchise have leaned into auto-battlers or strategy-lite mechanics, Fates returns to the series' roots. It is a first-person, open-world RPG designed to immerse players in a high-fantasy setting with a level of visual fidelity rarely seen on handheld devices.

Exploration-First Design: Players are encouraged to wander through diverse biomes, from sprawling emerald forests to desolate volcanic reaches, discovering hidden dungeons and ancient lore.

Tactical Real-Time Combat: The game blends the strategic "party-based" feel of the classics with modern action elements, requiring players to manage spells, stamina, and positioning.

Deep Character Progression: You aren't just playing a hero; you are crafting a legend. With a robust skill tree and gear system, the "Fates" of your party members are entirely in your hands.

Why It Matters for the Franchise
Might & Magic: Fates isn't just another mobile game; it is a strategic move by Ubisoft to modernize the IP. By partnering with Netflix Games, the title aims to provide a premium experience without the aggressive monetization often found in mobile RPGs. It seeks to bridge the gap between the "old school" grid-based movement of the 90s and the fluid, expansive worlds of modern RPGs like The Elder Scrolls.
