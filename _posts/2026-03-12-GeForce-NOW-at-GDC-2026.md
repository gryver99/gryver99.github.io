## GeForce NOW at GDC 2026: Cloud VR Hits 90 FPS on Mobile and Headsets 

NVIDIA has dropped a major technical bombshell at the 2026 Game Developers Conference. As part of its latest GeForce NOW update, the company announced that cloud-based Virtual Reality (VR) streaming now supports up to 90 FPS, a significant leap from the previous 60 FPS cap. This update aims to eliminate motion sickness and provide a "native-like" experience for mobile VR users.

The "90 FPS" Breakthrough
For VR, frame rate is not just about smoothness—it’s about comfort. By increasing the streaming ceiling to 90 FPS, NVIDIA is meeting the native refresh rate of most modern headsets, drastically reducing latency and the "screen-door effect" often associated with cloud streaming.

Availability: Exclusive to GeForce NOW Ultimate members.

Rollout Date: The update is scheduled to go live starting March 19, 2026.

Visual Fidelity: Support for up to 4K resolution per eye (on compatible devices like Vision Pro) using NVIDIA’s CloudXR technology.

Supported Devices
The 90 FPS update is specifically optimized for high-end mobile VR headsets and tablets:

Apple Vision Pro: Now supports up to 4K at 90 FPS (and up to 120 FPS in specific cinematic modes).

Meta Quest Series (Quest 3/Pro): Full 90 FPS support for PCVR titles streamed via the cloud.

Pico 4 & 5: Optimized streaming profiles for ultra-low latency.

Android Mobile: High-end smartphones (like the Red Magic 11 Pro) acting as "bridge" devices for VR glasses (like XREAL or Lenovo Legion Glasses).

New Ecosystem Features
Beyond the FPS boost, NVIDIA announced several quality-of-life updates for the platform:

GOG Integration: Soon, users will be able to link their GOG.com accounts, allowing for seamless library syncing and DRM-free game streaming.

Smart Account Labels: The GeForce NOW app now features visual tags on game tiles to identify which titles are available through linked subscriptions like Xbox Game Pass and Ubisoft+.

DLSS 4.5 Dynamic Frame Gen: This new AI technology will be integrated into the cloud servers to help maintain that 90 FPS target even in graphically demanding titles like CONTROL Resonant and The Witcher 4.

Why it Matters
This update effectively turns a standalone headset (like a Quest 3) into a high-end PCVR rig without the need for a physical computer or cables. By leveraging the power of RTX 50-series servers in the cloud, NVIDIA is making AAA VR gaming accessible to anyone with a fast 5G or Wi-Fi 7 connection.
